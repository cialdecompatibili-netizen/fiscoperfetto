<p>Did you realise you forgot an income, or did not file your return? The right moment to fix it is <strong>before</strong> a letter arrives from the Revenue Agency. The ravvedimento operoso (voluntary correction) lets you correct with reduced penalties, but time matters a lot. Here are the main rules, in simple terms.</p><h2 id="two-cases">Two different cases</h2><ul><li><strong>You did not file the return</strong>: failure to file.</li><li><strong>You filed it but with errors or omissions</strong>: you need an amended return (dichiarazione integrativa).</li></ul><h2 id="ninety">Return not filed: the 90-day rule</h2><p>If the return is filed <strong>within 90 days of the ordinary deadline</strong>, it is treated as <strong>late but valid</strong>: you pay a penalty that can be reduced through ravvedimento operoso, plus any taxes and interest due. If you go beyond this deadline, the return is treated as <strong>omitted</strong>: ravvedimento operoso is no longer allowed to cure the delay and penalties are much heavier.</p><p>For example, for the 2026 VAT return due on 30 April the 90 days ran out on 29 July 2026. For each return the 90 days count from its ordinary deadline, so check yours.</p><h2 id="amended">Return filed with errors: the amended return</h2><p>An amended return corrects a return already filed and valid. It can be in your favour (more credit, less tax) or against you (more tax to pay). Deadlines and penalties depend on the type of correction and when you file: if the correction means more tax, you must also pay tax, interest and penalties reduced through ravvedimento.</p><h2 id="what-to-do">What to do now</h2><ol><li><strong>Identify the return</strong> concerned and its ordinary deadline.</li><li><strong>Count the days</strong>: are they fewer than 90, or more?</li><li><strong>Work out what is due</strong>: tax, legal interest and reduced penalty.</li><li><strong>File the return</strong> (late or amended) and pay with F24 and the correct tax codes.</li><li><strong>Keep</strong> the filing and payment receipts.</li></ol><h2 id="mistakes">Mistakes to avoid</h2><ul><li>Waiting: the deadlines run every day and, once 90 days have passed, ravvedimento for the delay is no longer possible.</li><li>Filing but not paying: the penalty for non-payment remains.</li><li>Using the wrong tax code or miscalculating interest.</li></ul><h2 id="faq">Frequently asked questions</h2><h3>After 90 days can I still file the return?</h3><p>Yes, but it is treated as omitted and much higher penalties apply, with no ravvedimento for the delay.</p><h3>Is an amended return always possible?</h3><p>It is possible to correct a valid return already filed. Deadlines and consequences depend on the type of correction.</p><h3>How much does ravvedimento cost?</h3><p>It depends on the tax, the days of delay and the type of violation: the penalty is reduced according to timing.</p><h3>Is it better to correct or wait?</h3><p>In general, correcting before receiving any notice gives more chance of reduced penalties.</p><h2 id="sources">Sources</h2><ul><li><a href="https://www.informazionefiscale.it/sanzioni-dichiarazione-IVA-omessa-tardiva-ravvedimento-operoso" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Informazione Fiscale, 2026 VAT return late or omitted: penalties and ravvedimento operoso, in Italian</a></li><li><a href="https://www.pmi.it/impresa/contabilita-e-fisco/486420/dichiarazione-tardiva-modello-redditi-sanzione-ridotta.html" rel="noopener" target="_blank">PMI.it, Late return: Modello Redditi with reduced penalty, in Italian</a></li><li><a href="https://edotto.com/articolo/global-minimum-tax-ravvedimento-e-adempimenti-tardivi-dopo-il-30-giugno-2026" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Edotto, ravvedimento and late filings: the 90-day deadline, in Italian</a></li></ul><div class="art-nota"><p>Tax rules change often and depend on your personal situation.
