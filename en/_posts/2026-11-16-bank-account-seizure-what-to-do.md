<p>Finding your account blocked is frightening, but seizure by the collection agency Agenzia delle entrate-Riscossione (AdER) follows precise rules and there are ways to defend yourself. According to press reports, AdER's 2026 operating plan foresees about 120,000 account seizures: it is not an abstract threat. Here is what to know.</p><h2>How it works</h2><p>AdER serves the order directly on the bank, without going through a judge: the procedure is provided by art. 72-bis of Presidential Decree 602/1973, which since 1 January 2026 has been merged into the Consolidated Collection Act (Legislative Decree 33/2025, art. 170). The bank freezes the sums and, after the set period, pays them to the collection agent.</p><h2>What to do right away</h2><ol><li><strong>Read the order</strong>: which tax bills it lists and for what amount.</li><li><strong>Check whether the debt is correct</strong> and whether it was already paid, rescheduled or cancelled.</li><li><strong>Consider instalments</strong>: from the request AdER cannot start new procedures, and by paying the first instalment procedures already under way may end if they have not reached an advanced stage.</li><li><strong>If there are defects</strong>: self-review request, suspension or opposition before the judge. Deadlines are tight, so do not wait.</li><li><strong>Check protected sums</strong>: salary and pension have limits, see our dedicated guide.</li></ol><h2>Mistakes to avoid</h2><ul><li>Moving money quickly to shield it: it can have criminal consequences too. A negotiated plan is better.</li><li>Not replying and hoping it goes away.</li><li>Paying amounts different from the order, or through channels not provided.</li></ul><h2>Frequently asked questions</h2><h3>Was I warned first?</h3><p>Usually the debt already has a tax bill and the 60 days have passed. Seizure on the account can arrive without a new warning.</p><h3>Can they take everything?</h3><p>Not always: salary and pension have limits. It depends on when they were credited.</p><h3>Can it be unblocked?</h3><p>It depends on the case: payment, instalments or challenging the order.</p><h2 id="sources">Sources</h2><ul><li><a href="https://www.money.it/controlli-fiscali-2026-il-piano-operativo-prevede-120000-pignoramenti-di-conti-corrente-entro-l-anno" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Money.it, Tax checks 2026: AdER operating plan (Italian)</a></li><li><a href="https://avvocaticartellesattoriali.com/2026/09/16/pignoramento-presso-terzi-ex-art-72-bis-dpr-n-602-1973-con-efficacia-esecutiva-limitata-a-60-giorni-guida-per-profili/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">AdER third-party seizure from 1 January 2026 (art. 170 Legislative Decree 33/2025), Italian</a></li><li><a href="https://www.fiscoetasse.com/new-rassegna-stampa/1576-rateizzazione-cartelle-quante-rate-puoi-fare-scoprilo-subito.html" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Fisco e Tasse, Tax bills 2026: effects of instalments (Italian)</a></li></ul><div class="art-nota"><p>Tax rules change often and depend on your personal situation.
