<p>E-invoices always go through the Exchange System (SDI), but the data you enter changes depending on who receives them. Getting the recipient wrong is among the most common mistakes.</p><h2>To a company (B2B)</h2><p>Enter the client's recipient code or certified email (PEC). If you do not have them, the invoice remains available in their tax drawer, but it is better to ask for them first.</p><h2>To a private individual (B2C)</h2><p>The invoice must still be issued and transmitted. You can give the client a readable copy, because they do not receive the XML. Use the recipient code made of seven zeros.</p><h2>To the public administration</h2><p>You need the office's six-character unique code, and split payment normally applies: the entity pays the VAT directly. The CIG and CUP codes must be stated when required.</p><h2>If you are flat-rate</h2><p>Flat-rate taxpayers are also subject to e-invoicing, with different adjustment timelines depending on revenue. Check your position before issuing on paper.</p><h2>How Fisco Perfetto helps you</h2><p>We explain your situation in plain words and tell you what to do, in what order and by which deadlines. Tax rules change often: before deciding, always check your case with a consultant.</p>
