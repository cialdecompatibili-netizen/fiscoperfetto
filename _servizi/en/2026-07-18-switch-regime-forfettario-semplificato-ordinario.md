<p>The flat-rate regime is simple, but it applies a fixed coefficient and does not let you deduct actual costs. If you spend a lot to work, the numbers may not add up.</p><h2>When flat-rate pays off</h2><p>With few costs and revenue under the threshold, the substitute tax is almost always advantageous and management is reduced to a minimum.</p><h2>When to look at the simplified regime</h2><p>If your costs exceed the share that the coefficient "allows", or if you buy goods on which you would like to deduct VAT, the simplified regime can leave you more net income, at the price of more obligations.</p><h2>How to change</h2><p>The choice of regime is communicated in the tax return and can be changed, but with timing and constraints. Always compare the two routes on a year of real numbers, not on an optimistic estimate.</p><div class="art-nota"><p>Ask for a side-by-side simulation with your revenue and costs: it takes a few minutes and sometimes makes a difference of hundreds of euro a year.</p></div>
