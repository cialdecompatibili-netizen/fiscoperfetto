<p>Going from employee to VAT number means saying goodbye to the thirteenth month, paid holidays and severance pay, but also gaining autonomy and potential. The right question is not "how much do I invoice", it is "how much do I keep".</p><h2>From gross to net</h2><p>Your net salary cannot be compared with turnover: from turnover you must subtract tax, INPS contributions, business costs and amounts to set aside for the holidays nobody pays for any more.</p><h2>What you no longer have</h2><p>No paid holidays or sick leave, no severance pay, no employer paying contributions for you. Work out a monthly amount to put aside to cover these gaps.</p><h2>The first year</h2><p>Those who start can access favourable conditions: a reduced tax in the flat-rate regime and dedicated contribution schemes. Check you meet the requirements, because some lapse if you keep working for your former employer.</p><div class="art-nota"><p>Rule of thumb: every month set aside a fixed percentage of what you collect for taxes and contributions. That way deadlines never catch you unprepared.</p></div>
